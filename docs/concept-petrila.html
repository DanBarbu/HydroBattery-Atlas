Why this site

Petrila is the deepest mine in the Jiu Valley (workings to ~940 m below a ~675 m ASL surface) and one of the deepest in Romania, closed in 2015 after 156 years of operation. Its surface headframe towers are preserved as historic monuments — a strong indicator that the main shafts have not yet been backfilled, unlike most of the basin's older closures. Depth is the entire game in underground PHES: at ~800 m of usable head, every cubic metre of accessible void stores roughly 2 kWh, so even the harsh void haircut applied below still leaves an Altân Tepe-class storage figure.

Risks and engineering burden

  • Methane. Gassy coal mine — the 2008 Petrila explosion killed 13. Ventilation/inertisation is a precondition for any underground works; residual gas management must be designed into the water cycle.
  • Acid mine water. Coal-basin water chemistry requires treatment or isolation of the cycling volume (encoded as a water-contamination flag in the Atlas record).
  • Weak rock. Coal measures, not hard rock: chamber stability for machine halls needs early geotechnical screening.
  • Heritage interface. The protected surface ensemble constrains surface works but also protects shaft access — likely a net positive if engaged early.

What the SNIMVJ closure dossier must confirm

  • Which shafts are open, their depths and lining condition;
  • Gallery/chamber volumes by level, and which levels are sealed by concrete closure dams;
  • Current mine-water level and pumping history (flooded levels reduce usable head);
  • Residual methane regime.
Caveats: town-level coordinates; depth and void figures are estimates pending the closure dossier; storage uses ~0.0024 GWh per Mm³ per metre of head. Treat every number on this sheet as a screening value with roughly a factor-2 band.
